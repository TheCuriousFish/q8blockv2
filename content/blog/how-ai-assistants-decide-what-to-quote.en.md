A growing number of people ask an AI assistant instead of opening a search engine. The question itself has barely changed: who does this service in this area, and are they any good. What changes is the shape of the answer. Instead of a list of links to choose from, the person gets a written answer that names businesses and gives details, and usually names the sources it took them from.

The practical question for a business owner is how the assistant decides what to quote.

## An assistant does not know you. It finds text about you

This is the most important point and the most overlooked. An AI assistant does not keep its own database of local businesses that it maintains by hand. When it is asked a local question, it looks for pages, reads them, then writes an answer from them and cites where it came from.

That means appearing in the answer has three conditions, and all three sit outside the AI itself. There has to be written text about you. That text has to be indexed so it can be reached. And it has to be clear enough that a sentence can be pulled out of it.

If you have no website, the first condition already fails. A business profile is data: a name, a number, an address, some reviews. An assistant may read it and give your name, but it finds no text there describing how you work, what the service covers, or why anyone would pick you. Those sentences only exist on pages.

## What makes a page quotable

### The fact has to exist as text

What gets extracted is text. A page that puts its services inside an image, a moving slider, or a separate PDF is carrying nothing anyone can take from it. This is a very common failure in websites built to look good and nothing else. Every piece of information that matters is a graphic.

### A short, direct answer under a clear question

The wording that gets quoted is usually a sentence that answers directly. A subheading phrased as a question, followed by two or three sentences that plainly answer it, is far easier to extract than three paragraphs circling the subject before they land on it.

### The scope has to be specific

Specific sentences get quoted. General ones get skipped. "We provide high quality services to all our customers" answers nothing. A sentence that says what the service covers, what it does not cover, and where it is delivered is a ready made answer to a real question.

### Your details have to match everywhere

Your name, address and phone number should be written in the same form on your website, on your profile, and in any directory you appear in. When those details disagree between sources, you are less likely to be cited, because the sources contradict each other. Consistency is not a cosmetic detail. It is what makes you one clear business rather than three near copies.

## What does not work

Stuffing keywords works no better here than it does in a search engine, and arguably worse. Stuffed text is hard to pull a clean sentence out of.

Very long pages with no subheadings are a problem too. A wall of text with no structure makes an answer harder to extract, not easier.

And big claims with nothing behind them add nothing. A sentence nobody can verify is not a sentence anybody wants to cite.

## A warning you should hear

Nobody controls what an AI assistant says, and there is no way to guarantee your name appears in an answer. Anyone selling you "guaranteed visibility in ChatGPT" is selling you something they do not own.

What you do control is the other side of the equation, and it is the side that actually matters. That there exists clear, indexed, specific text saying what you do and where you do it. That is all anyone can honestly offer, and it is the same work that gets your pages found in Google search. The two are closer than they look. A clear, well written page is the answer in both cases.

## The simplest way to start

Write down the ten questions you get asked on the phone every week. Make each one a subheading on the right service page, and write the answer underneath it in your own voice, in three sentences, with no exaggeration.

That is text that is useful to your customer, understandable to a search engine, and quotable by an AI assistant, all out of one piece of work.
