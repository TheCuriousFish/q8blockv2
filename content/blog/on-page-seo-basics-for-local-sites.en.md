A customer opens the "Services" page on an AC company's website and finds ten different services crammed into one paragraph, with no answer to his actual question: a central AC unit that has stopped cooling. He goes back to the search results and opens a competitor's page instead. That is exactly what **on-page SEO** deals with, everything inside the page itself, its text, headings and images, as opposed to what sits outside it, like links from other sites. A page built with the right basics answers the customer's question before he thinks about leaving, and that is what all of these basics ultimately come down to.

## One page answers one question

The first rule of on-page SEO is that every page answers one question, not every question your business could ever get asked. A "central AC maintenance" page is not the same as a "window AC installation" page, even if the same technician and the same team handle both. When one page tries to answer everything, it answers nothing in any depth, and the reader feels he is reading a generic text that was not written for him, so he leaves to find something more precise.

This does not mean your site needs a separate page for every small detail, only for every real service people actually ask about, and that is what should decide the number, not a wish for more pages.

## The title and first heading speak the customer's words

The page title and the first heading need to say the service and the place in the customer's own words, not the company's internal phrasing. "Central AC maintenance in Farwaniya" works better than "Our air conditioning services," because it matches almost exactly what the searcher typed.

The first paragraph under that heading needs to answer the question immediately, in two or three sentences, before any introduction to the company, its history or its mission. Someone who reaches your page wants an answer, not an introduction to who you are. If you have something to say about your business, that belongs in a later paragraph, not the first one.

## What the body of the page needs

After the direct answer, the body of the page needs four things to be complete.

- **What the service covers exactly**, without vagueness or loose phrases like "comprehensive service."
- **How the service is done, step by step**, in the customer's words, not the trade's, because people hesitate over what they do not understand.
- **What the service does not cover**, because that saves you calls that do not suit you and brings forward the ones that do.
- **What happens when the customer calls**: does a technician come to inspect first, is a price set over the phone, how soon can someone reach him.

Each of these four points answers a question already sitting in the reader's mind before he picks up the phone, and the more of them the page answers, the further along the call starts instead of from zero.

## Name, address and phone in the same form everywhere

Your business name, address and phone number need to be written in the same form on every page of your site, on your Google profile, and in any other directory that mentions you. When the form differs from one place to another, a number written with different spacing or an address written two different ways, it becomes harder for Google and for AI assistants to confirm you are one real business rather than several conflicting copies. Pick one form and use it everywhere from today onward.

## Images and speed decide if the page gets read at all

A customer opens three search results at once and starts with whichever one is ready first. A slow page loses before a single line of it is read, usually because of oversized images uploaded exactly as they were taken on a phone, with no resizing.

You do not need to understand code to check this. Open your page on your own phone, on mobile data rather than office wifi, on an inner page rather than only the homepage, and see how long you wait before the text and the number appear. Every second of waiting is a chance for your customer to open a different page instead.

## What makes a page quotable

AI assistants read written pages and quote direct sentences from them, so a page needs to be clear text, not images or separate files carrying the important information. What gets quoted best is a subheading phrased as a question, followed by two or three sentences that answer it directly, with a specific detail that can be verified.

A general sentence like "we provide the best service" gets quoted by nobody, because it says nothing real that can be checked. A long page with no clear subheadings makes the answer harder to extract, not easier, for a search engine and for an AI assistant alike.

## Where to start

Pick one page you already have and ask it this question: does it answer one clear question from the very first sentence? If the answer is no, start there, one page at a time, rather than trying to fix the whole site at once. The "How it works" section on the homepage shows how these pages get built in more detail.
