When we explain that a website will have a dedicated page for every service and every area, the same question always comes back. Why all these pages? My business is one business, and all my services are written on one page already. Is that not enough?

The answer is that your business really is one business. Your customers' questions are not one question.

## A search is a sentence, not a category

Look at what people actually type when they need a service. It is rarely one word. They type the specific service they want, they add the place they are in, and often a third detail: the type of building, the size of the job, when they need it, or a particular problem they want solved.

Every one of those combinations is a different question. And when a search engine compares a page that answers that exact question with a page that mentions the service on one line inside a list, the outcome is neither mysterious nor unfair.

The page that tries to answer every question answers none of them. It names the services without going into any of them, so it drops out of the running for the long sentence, which is exactly the sentence typed by the customer who is closest to calling.

## What a service page actually carries

A good service page is not a paragraph about the service. It is a complete answer to one question, and it usually holds:

The scope, precisely. What is included and what is not. That alone saves you calls that do not suit you and brings forward the ones that do.

How the work is done, step by step, in the customer's words and not the trade's. People hesitate over what they do not understand.

What the customer needs to have ready before the appointment. That one piece of information does more for your credibility than any marketing sentence.

The questions you are asked on the phone every day, written out with their answers. You already know them. Writing them down costs you nothing.

A clear way to get in touch, somewhere the person who read to the end can see it.

## And why a page per area

For the same reason, plus one more. A customer searching for a service in his area wants to know you serve his area. The general line "we serve all areas" does not reassure him, because every website says it.

A page built for an area answers both questions at once: yes we come here, and this is the service as it is delivered here specifically. When a customer sees the name of his own area in the heading of a page, he stops searching.

## The difference between a built page and a duplicated one

This is where most websites fail, and it needs to be clear before you pay anyone for "a hundred pages".

If those pages are a hundred copies of one text with the area name swapped out, you do not have a hundred pages. You have one page repeated a hundred times, and search engines treat it that way. Worse, the reader works it out in two seconds. He reads a generic paragraph that says nothing about where he lives, and goes back to the results.

A built page says something true and specific about this service in this place. It might be the kind of buildings there, the problem that comes up most often in that area, or what is different about doing the work there. The test is practical. If you deleted the area name from the page, would anything left in the text show it was written for that place? If the answer is no, it is a duplicate.

## What these pages do for each other

These pages have a second effect that most owners never see. Service pages and area pages link to each other, which gives a search engine a clear structure it can read what you do and where you do it from. A one page website has no structure. It has a surface.

They have a third effect that shows up in the calls themselves. Somebody who reaches you through a service page has read what that service covers before he picked up the phone, so the call starts further along. You are not explaining from zero. You are agreeing the details.

## The point

The number of pages is not a goal in itself and nobody should sell it to you as one. The number is the natural result of a simple question: how many different questions do your customers ask before they call? Write the services you genuinely offer in one column and the areas you genuinely reach in another, and you will know how many pages your website needs, and why.
