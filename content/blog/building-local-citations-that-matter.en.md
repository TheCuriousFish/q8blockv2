A business owner searched his own company name in Google to see what came up, and found an old listing from a directory he had forgotten years ago, carrying his previous address and a phone number he stopped using a long time back. He never wrote that listing himself, and he has no idea who did, but it is sitting there right now saying something about his business that is completely different from what his website or his Google profile says.

That listing is a citation, and a citation is simpler than it looks. Knowing the difference between the kind worth fixing and the kind that is not worth your time at all saves a lot of wasted effort.

## What a citation is, in one plain sentence

A citation is your business name, address and phone number, written on a site that is not your own: a business directory, a maps platform, a page on a delivery platform, or any other page that mentions your business along with its details. There does not need to be a link in it, and no article written about you. Just your details, sitting in a place you do not control.

## Why consistency is the whole point

Google does not know that all these listings are talking about the same business unless their details match exactly. A phone number written with a different spacing, a street name abbreviated in one place and spelled out in full in another, an old address sitting next to a new one: every one of these mismatches, however small it looks, makes Google hesitate over a real question. Is this one business, or two similar businesses operating in the same trade? That hesitation always works against you, never for you. Being identical everywhere is what makes you one clear entity in the eyes of both search engines and AI assistants.

## Which listings are worth your time, and which are noise

Not every citation carries the same value, and that difference is worth knowing before you spend time or money on it. Only two kinds are worth the effort. The listings a real customer in your city actually uses when he searches for your service or compares his options, and your own profiles on the platforms you already deal with, such as the maps or delivery platforms your business already runs on today. The directories that mass produce thousands of listings at once, with not a single real visitor ever opening them, and that exist mainly to sell you a large number with no real benefit behind it, are noise. They carry your details without carrying a single potential customer along with them. A handful of listings real people actually see is worth more than a hundred nobody opens.

## A citation is not a backlink

This is a common mix-up that needs to end today. A citation is not a backlink, and it should never be sold to you as one. A backlink is a site that points to your website with written content around it, and it carries a different kind of value entirely. A citation is your three details and nothing more, no text around it and no opinion in it. Both are useful in their own way, but anybody who offers you a hundred citations and describes them as a hundred backlinks is mixing up two very different things, to their own advantage.

## How to audit what already exists, and fix what is wrong

Search your business name in Google, and look past the first page of results too, since old listings tend to hide there. Note every listing you find carrying your details, no matter how small or old it looks, and compare what it says against your current profile, field by field: the name, the address, the phone number. Old listings usually come from an address you have since moved from, or a number you stopped using and forgot was ever written down anywhere. When you find one that is wrong, look for a "suggest an edit" option on that page, or contact the site directly to fix it, and do not leave it as it is just because it looks old or unlikely to be seen. It is still speaking your name every time it shows up in front of a customer comparing two results.

## Where to start: citations are a floor, not a growth engine

Start with your Google profile, because it is the most important citation of all. Then check your profiles on the platforms you actually deal with and make sure they are current. After that, search your own name and fix everything that is wrong or outdated. Leave the large, random directories aside, because they are not worth the time. Most important of all, know the limits of this work. Matching citations are a solid floor you stand on, not an engine that pushes you forward on its own. A solid floor is a necessary condition for everything built on top of it, but on its own it is not what grows your business.
