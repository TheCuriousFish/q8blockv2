A Google Business Profile is free, it is yours, and it is the first thing a potential customer sees about you. Even so, most businesses use less than half of it, usually because somebody set it up in one afternoon and never went back.

What follows is work you can do yourself in one sitting without paying anybody. We do not ask for access to our clients' profiles. We hand them a checklist to work through, and that comes from the same principle: the profile should stay in the hands of the person who owns it.

## Start with the primary category

The primary category is the strongest thing you control inside the profile, and the thing most often chosen carelessly. The rule is simple. Pick the category that describes what you actually do most of the time, not the broadest thing you could claim.

A broad category feels cleverer because it covers more, but it puts you up against businesses nothing like yours and makes Google's understanding of you less precise. Everything else you offer belongs in the secondary categories, which exist for exactly that.

## Use your real name and nothing else

Do not add descriptive words or area names to your business name in the profile. It is against Google's rules explicitly, a competitor can report it at any time, and the outcome is a suspended or forcibly edited profile you spent years building.

Your business name is the name on your signage and your paperwork. Being found is built from the categories, the services and the text on your website, not from padding the name.

## Fill in every service, with names and descriptions

This is the section left empty most often. For every service you offer, add a separate entry named the way your customer says it rather than the way the trade says it, and give it a short description saying what it covers and what it does not.

It is the cheapest way to widen what Google understands about you, and it is within reach right now.

## Set the service areas or the address, not both without thinking

If customers come to you at a fixed address, keep the address visible and make sure the map pin actually sits on the right entrance, not in the middle of the building or on the next street.

If you go to the customer, set the service areas you genuinely reach. Stretching them across the whole city does not widen your visibility as much as people imagine, and it brings you calls from places you do not actually serve.

## Opening hours, especially the exceptions

Wrong opening hours lose you calls in silence. A visitor who sees "closed now" does not call and leaves no trace.

The part that matters most is the special hours: holidays, closures and the days you shut early. Google lets you set those in advance, and spending five minutes on it a week before a holiday prevents losing a whole week.

## Questions and answers, the abandoned section

The profile has a questions and answers section anyone can write in, and the business owner can post a question and answer it himself.

Most people do not know that, so the section sits empty or fills up with random unanswered questions. You already know the questions you are asked on the phone every day. Write five of them with their answers. It takes five minutes, and it means the person reading your profile finds his answer instead of calling a competitor to ask.

## Reviews: ask at the right moment, reply to all of them

The best time to ask for a review is right after the work is finished while you are still with the customer, not two days later by message. Ask out loud, and open the link for him if that helps.

Then reply to every review, the good ones and the bad ones. A reply to a negative review, two calm and factual sentences, is not really addressed to the reviewer. It is addressed to everyone who reads it afterwards. Anybody who reads a bad review reads your reply immediately after it, and judges how you work from that.

## Keep your details identical everywhere

Your name, address and phone number should be written in exactly the same form on the profile, on your website, and in any directory or page that mentions you. A different address format, a number written with a different spacing, two different numbers in two places: each one makes you less clear to search engines and to AI assistants.

Pick one form, save it in a file, and use it everywhere from today onwards.

## Keep ownership of the profile

Do not transfer ownership of the profile to any company or person. If somebody needs to work on it, give them manager access, which is enough for everything they need. Keep the owner account in your name and on your email.

Ownership is the difference between an asset you have and an asset you are borrowing.

## And finally, know its limits

Everything above is worth your time and it is the floor your business stands on in Google. But that floor has a ceiling. The profile appears inside a narrow radius around your address, and it has no room for the detail a customer wants before he calls.

Do what is on this page first, because it is free, it is quick and it is yours. Then ask yourself where the customer goes when he wants to know more.
