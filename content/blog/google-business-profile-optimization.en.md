A cleaning business owner opens Google Maps to see how he looks to a customer searching in his area, and finds the profile there: a name, a number, a handful of photos, decent reviews. He assumes the job is done. But the profile in front of him is using less than half of what it can actually do, and the empty half is usually the part that would have brought him customers who do not know he exists yet.

The word "optimization" here does not mean a trick or a technical secret. It simply means filling the profile in so Google understands precisely what you do and where you do it. That understanding is what decides whether your profile even shows up when somebody searches for you, before anything else comes into it.

## What "optimizing" actually means here

Your Google profile is built from fields: the name, the category, the services, the hours, the photos, the posts, the reviews. A small number of those fields decide how many times your profile appears in search results at all. The larger number do not change how often you appear in the slightest. They only change how you look once you have already appeared. Confusing the two is why so much of a business owner's time goes into the wrong fields: hours picking photos, and minutes on the category that decides who sees them in the first place.

## What decides how often you appear

- **The primary category and the secondary categories.** Pick the one that describes what you actually do most of the time, not the broadest thing you could claim. A broad category feels smarter because it covers more, but it puts you up against businesses nothing like yours and makes Google's understanding of you less precise.
- **The service list, with a real description on each one.** Do not stop at naming the service. Write a short description for each one saying what it covers and what it does not. This is the cheapest way to widen what Google understands about you.
- **The service area or the address.** If customers come to a fixed address, make sure the map pin sits on the right entrance, not the middle of the building or the street next door. If you go to the customer instead, set the areas you genuinely reach rather than the whole city, because stretching it without reason does not widen your visibility as much as people imagine, and it brings you calls from places you do not actually serve.
- **Opening hours, especially the exceptions.** Holidays and early closing days need to be set in advance, so a visitor never sees "closed now" for no real reason, closes the page, and leaves no trace at all.
- **Your name, address and phone number, in the same form everywhere.** Any difference between them, even one character or a number written with different spacing, makes Google less confident that all these sources are describing the same business.

## What only changes how you look

Once your profile is already appearing, the fields that decide whether the person who sees you picks you come into play: real photos of your work, your logo, your cover image, and short posts. These matter and deserve their own time, but they do not put your profile in front of more people. They make somebody who already saw you trust you faster and choose you sooner, and that is a real difference. It is just a difference inside the group who already saw you, not a difference in how many people see you in the first place.

## Reviews: what they actually do

Reviews sit in the same part of the picture. They work mostly on people who already see your profile, making them more likely to pick you over the name sitting next to you in the list. They do much less to widen how many people see you to begin with. Ask for a review right after the work is finished, while you are still with the customer, not by a message two days later. Then reply to every review, the good ones and the bad ones. A calm, factual two sentence reply to a negative review is not really addressed to the person who wrote it. It is addressed to everyone who reads it afterwards and judges how you handle things from it.

## The ceiling on the profile

Everything above is worth your time, but it has a ceiling that no amount of work inside the profile can lift, no matter how well you do it. The profile is tied to one address, so the circle you appear in is drawn around it. Somebody two districts away, typing the exact words your neighbour types, sees a completely different set of results, no matter how well your profile is optimized. The profile also runs on one category and a short services list trying to stand in for everything you actually offer, while the customer searches in a long, specific sentence that a category name cannot answer. And there is no room in it for the detail that explains how a service is actually done or exactly what it covers for a situation like his. ## Where to start in practice

Start with the primary category, and make sure it describes what you actually do rather than the broadest thing you could claim. Add every service with its own name and its own description. Set the service area or the address precisely. Check the opening hours and set the upcoming exceptions a week ahead of time. Make your name, address and phone number identical everywhere they appear online. Only after all of that move on to photos, posts, and asking for reviews. That order is the real difference between half an hour spent where it matters and hours spent somewhere that changes nothing at all.
